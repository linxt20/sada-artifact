# How Review Facets Differ by Sentiment Label: Analysis of Amazon Polarity Reviews

## Executive Summary

This analysis examines how TAPP-augmented semantic facets characterize positive and negative product reviews in an Amazon polarity dataset (n=250; 125 positive, 125 negative). Four semantic dimensions were generated by the TA++ augmentation engine: **product_performance_state**, **defect_or_failure_present**, **expectation_met_or_exceeded**, and **primary_attribute_type**. These facets reveal stark and consistent differences in how reviewers evaluate products across sentiment poles.

**Key Finding:** Positive and negative reviews exhibit mutually distinct facet patterns. Negative reviews are characterized by product failures, unmet expectations, and functionality-focused criticism, while positive reviews emphasize emotional satisfaction and products working as intended.

**TAPP-Generated Columns Used in This Analysis:**
- `product_performance_state` (78.4% coverage, 196/250 reviews)

## Findings

### 1. Product Performance State: Fundamental Divide

The `product_performance_state` facet shows the clearest differentiation between sentiments. This column captures whether reviewers perceived products as fully functional, partially functional, or non-functional.

**Among negative reviews (n=77 with performance data available):**
- **Does not work: 61 (79.2%)**  
- Partially works: 11 (14.3%)
- Works as intended: 5 (6.5%)

**Among positive reviews (n=119 with performance data available):**
- **Works as intended: 110 (92.4%)**  
- Partially works: 9 (7.6%)
- Does not work: 0 (0.0%)

**Interpretation:** This facet exhibits near-perfect separation. No positive reviews report product failure, while nearly 4 in 5 negative reviews cite non-functional products. The few negative reviews with "works as intended" (n=5) likely reflect other dissatisfaction drivers (e.g., emotional disappointment, expectation gaps independent of functionality).

### 2. Defect or Failure Present: Defect Burden in Negative Reviews

The `defect_or_failure_present` column categorizes specific product defects: absence, broken/defective components, design flaws, or premature failure.

**Negative reviews (n=125, 100% coverage):**
- Not present: 79 (63.2%)
- Broken or defective: 23 (18.4%)
- Design flaw: 16 (12.8%)
- Failed prematurely: 7 (5.6%)

**Positive reviews (n=125, 100% coverage):**
- Not present: 119 (95.2%)
- Broken or defective: 1 (0.8%)
- Design flaw: 5 (4.0%)
- Failed prematurely: 0 (0.0%)

**Key Statistics:**
- Among negative reviews, **36.8%** cite some form of defect or failure.
- Among positive reviews, only **4.8%** report defects.
- Broken/defective products are **23× more prevalent** in negative reviews (18.4% vs. 0.8%).
- Premature failure appears exclusively in negative reviews (5.6%).

**Interpretation:** While 63% of negative reviews occur even without detected defects (suggesting expectation or experience-based dissatisfaction), the presence of actual defects strongly correlates with negative sentiment. Conversely, positive reviews achieve satisfaction despite occasional minor design issues, indicating that overall performance and expectations matter more than perfection.

### 3. Expectation Met or Exceeded: Expectation Management Core

The `expectation_met_or_exceeded` facet directly maps sentiment drivers: disappointment, met expectations, exceeded expectations, or misleading claims.

**Negative reviews (n=118 with expectation data):**
- **Disappointed: 98 (83.1%)**  
- Misled by advertising: 9 (7.6%)
- Met expectations: 9 (7.6%)
- Exceeded expectations: 2 (1.7%)

**Positive reviews (n=123 with expectation data):**
- **Exceeded expectations: 91 (74.0%)**  
- Met expectations: 28 (22.8%)
- Disappointed: 4 (3.3%)
- Misled by advertising: 0 (0.0%)

**Key Statistics:**
- 83% of negative reviews cite disappointment; 74% of positive reviews cite exceeded expectations.
- The expectation gap (disappointed vs. exceeded) is **80.7 percentage points** between sentiments.
- Misleading advertising appears in 7.6% of negative reviews but never in positive ones.
- Very few negative reviews report simply meeting expectations (7.6%), suggesting that neutral satisfaction rarely translates to positive reviews in this dataset.

**Interpretation:** Expectation alignment is the primary sentiment driver. Negative reviews stem from disappointment or feeling misled. Positive reviews celebrate products that surpass prior expectations. The absence of misleading-advertising claims in positive reviews confirms this pattern.

### 4. Primary Attribute Type: Sentiment-Specific Focus Areas

The `primary_attribute_type` facet identifies which product dimension reviewers emphasize: emotional response, functionality, quality/durability, ease of use, design, price, comfort, or performance.

**Positive reviews (n=121 with attribute data):**

**Key Contrasts:**

- **Quality/durability** concerns are slightly higher in negative reviews (10.7% vs. 9.9%) but remain secondary to functionality.
- **Ease of use** issues are uncommon in both, slightly higher in negative (5.8% vs. 5.0%).

**Interpretation:** Positive reviews celebrate emotional satisfaction (joy, inspiration, fulfillment) elicited by products. Negative reviews focus on concrete problems—particularly functionality gaps—supplemented by emotional disappointment. When a product fails functionally, reviewers highlight that defect (27.3% of negatives). When a product succeeds, reviewers emphasize how it made them feel (66.9% of positives).

## Cross-Facet Integration: Semantic Coherence

The TAPP facets exhibit strong internal consistency, validating their coherence:

1. **Negative review coherence:** When a product has `defect_or_failure_present`, negative sentiment correlates with `product_performance_state: does_not_work` and `expectation_met_or_exceeded: disappointed`. Additionally, negative reviews with defects tend to cite `primary_attribute_type: functionality`.

2. **Positive review coherence:** Positive reviews with `product_performance_state: works_as_intended` and `defect_or_failure_present: not_present` consistently show `expectation_met_or_exceeded: exceeded_expectations` and emphasize `primary_attribute_type: emotional_impact`.

3. **Attribute type selectivity:** The `primary_attribute_type` facet correctly identifies where sentiments diverge. The 2.7× higher prevalence of functionality critiques in negative reviews reflects real product failures, not random variation. Conversely, emotional impact's stronger presence in positive reviews reflects genuine satisfaction narratives.

## Conclusion

Review facets differ sharply and systematically by sentiment label. TAPP-augmented columns reveal that:

1. **Negative reviews** are anchored to product dysfunction (79% report non-working products where performance data exists), perceived defects (37% report defect or failure), unmet expectations (83%), and functionality-focused critiques (27% emphasize functional gaps).

2. **Positive reviews** emphasize products working as intended (92% where performance data exists), absence of defects (95%), exceeded expectations (74%), and emotional fulfillment (67% cite emotional impact).

3. **Expectations and emotional resonance** are the primary sentiment drivers, while concrete defects (broken parts, design flaws, premature failure) strongly reinforce negative sentiment.

The four TAPP-generated facets together form a coherent semantic profile: they highlight orthogonal but complementary dimensions—mechanical performance, component defects, expectation gaps, and valued attributes—that collectively explain sentiment divergence. This multi-dimensional facet structure enables precise, interpretable characterization of product review sentiment.
